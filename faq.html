Frequently asked

Does sibni store or see my data?

No. You write directly from your phone to the tag using a free app. There's no sibni account, no server, and no upload step involved — we have no way to see what's on any tag.

Is the data on the tag protected or encrypted?

No — and that's intentional. Anyone with an NFC-capable phone can tap and read the tag, without needing an app or account. That's what makes it useful in an emergency to a stranger. It also means you should only put on it what you'd be comfortable with a stranger seeing.

What phones can read it?

Almost any phone from the last decade or so with NFC — most modern iPhones and Android phones. No app is needed to read a tag, only to write one.

Can I change what's written later?

Yes, as many times as you like — unless you've chosen to lock the tag. Locking makes it permanently read-only. See the how-to guide for details before doing that.

What happens if I lock it by mistake, or change my mind?

Locking is physically irreversible — there's no way for us or anyone else to undo it. If you're not fully sure, don't lock the tag yet. You can lock it later once you are.

Are the tags waterproof?

The 3D-printed shells are built to handle everyday exposure — rain, sweat, washing machines by accident — but they aren't rated for submersion. Avoid prolonged soaking.

Is this a substitute for a medical alert bracelet?

Not necessarily — some situations (certain medical conditions, some countries' regulations) call for a recognized medical ID specifically. Sibni tags are a general-purpose "reach my emergency contact / here's what a helper should know" tool, not a certified medical device. If you rely on a specific medical ID standard, keep using that alongside or instead of a sibni tag as appropriate.

What chip is inside?

An NXP NTAG216 with 888 bytes of usable storage — enough for a full contact card plus a short note.
